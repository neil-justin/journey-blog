Writing this from inside the year it describes. I'm in 4th year at AISAT College now, first semester, the last stretch before graduation.

The courses read differently after two jobs. Software Engineering 1 is the clearest case: requirements, design, process, quality. In first year those would've been diagrams to memorize for a quiz. Now they describe things I've actually done — schemas and API contracts other people built against, code review, release management. SE 1 is the first course I arrived at with scar tissue, and it cuts both ways: the theory names practices I improvised, and the experience tells me which parts of the textbook are load-bearing and which are ceremony. (Some are ceremony. Nobody at either company ever drew one.)

This semester's load: Software Engineering 1, Human-Computer Interaction 2, CS Thesis Writing 1, Social Issues and Professional Practice. HCI 102 is a quiet favorite after building for real users and sitting across 55 interview candidates. Thesis Writing 1 is the mountain, and the course that most resembles my OJT era: long project, no scaffolding, no test cases, a committee instead of a grader. My proposal draft is due soon and the title page is the only finished part.

Two things are true at once. I'm far ahead of where 1st-year me could've imagined — engineer, interim lead, all while finishing the degree. And I'm not done: Software Engineering 2 and Thesis Writing 2 are next semester, and the last stretch is where discipline matters most and energy is lowest. I know that pattern from shipping. I'm applying it here.

One post remains: the final reflection.
