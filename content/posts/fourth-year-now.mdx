# 4th Year — Where I Am Now

This post is different from the others: it is being written from inside the year it describes. I am in 4th year at AISAT College now — first semester, the final stretch before graduation.

## Studying what I have already been doing

Fourth year's courses read differently after two real jobs. Software Engineering 1 is the clearest example: requirements, design, process, quality — in first year these would have been abstract diagrams to memorize. Now they are a description of things I have actually done. I have specified database schemas, API contracts, and component breakdowns that other engineers built against. I have reviewed code, managed releases, onboarded engineers. SE 1 is the first course I have taken where I arrived with production scar tissue.

That is a strange and valuable position, and it cuts both ways. The theory gives my experience structure I did not have — names for practices I improvised, and discipline where I was winging it. Meanwhile the experience keeps the theory honest: I know which parts of the textbook are load-bearing and which are ceremony. The two educations keep correcting each other, which is exactly what this blog's final reflection will be about.

## The courses

The semester's load — Software Engineering 1, Human-Computer Interaction 2, CS Thesis Writing 1, and Social Issues and Professional Practice — is the degree turning toward completion.

HCI 102 is a quiet favorite. After building an admin dashboard and a workforce platform for real users, and after roughly 55 interviews spent watching how people actually think, Human-Computer Interaction is not theory to me — it is the formal study of what I kept learning by accident: interfaces are conversations, and most of them are badly held.

CS Thesis Writing 1 is the year's mountain. The thesis is the last major academic test — original work, defended, in writing. It is also, honestly, the course that most resembles my OJT era: a long project with no provided scaffolding, no test cases, and a committee instead of a grader. I suspect my thesis work and my work experience will keep trading techniques back and forth all year.

## The honest version

Two things are true at once, and this post should say both.

First: I am far ahead of where 1st-year me could have imagined. I have worked as a software engineer and an interim lead at real US companies while completing this degree. The distance between "hello world" and leading engineers is the whole journey this blog documents.

Second: I am not done, and the remaining work is real. Second semester brings Software Engineering 2 and CS Thesis Writing 2, plus the rest of the final subjects. The last stretch of a degree, like the last stretch of any long project, is where discipline matters most and energy is lowest. I know that pattern from shipping. I am applying it here.

## What this year means

Fourth year is the year the two halves of my journey fully merge — the student and the engineer finally working on the same person. The subjects are finishing my foundation; the work experience gave me the floor to stand on while finishing it.

One post remains after this: the final reflection on the whole trip, and what four years plus one career actually taught me. It is the closing chapter, and I already know its title: two educations.
