# First Job — Career Team LLC

In May 2025, while still working through my degree, I joined Career Team LLC as a software engineer — remote, for a US company. The professional phase of my journey didn't wait for graduation.

The shock was the codebase. A multi-tenant workforce platform — NestJS, Next.js, MongoDB — serving thousands of users across government agencies and school organizations. Three things hit me fast.

Scale is invisible until you inherit it. Every query I wrote touched data belonging to many organizations, so every feature carried a question school never asked: whose data is this, and who must never see it?

Code is read far more often than it is written. In school, a grader reads your work once. On a 15-person cross-functional team, my code was read by reviewers, extended by teammates, and revisited by me months later. Writing for that audience is a different skill.

Shipped software keeps living. Assignments end. Features don't — they get used, misused, and reworked.

The work itself settled into real ownership: the multi-tenant admin dashboard (and fixing its slow MongoDB queries), admin profile management, configurable workforce forms used by client organizations. I facilitated daily standups, bridging developers and product to keep sprint goals on track. I conducted technical interviews and buddied two new engineers through onboarding — being the person who explains a system turns out to be the fastest way to genuinely understand it.

The gap between knowing things and relying on them is what this job closed. The next chapter tested it harder: Thorne asked me to lead.
