# Leading Without a Title — Thorne Consulting

In March 2026 I joined Thorne Consulting, a US career-readiness startup, as the second engineer on the founding team. I arrived as a software engineer. By the time my contract ended in September 2026, I had held the interim lead role. The label arrived after the work did — which is the first thing this chapter taught me.

On a founding team there's no one to route hard questions to. Being engineer #2 meant my fingerprints ended up on everything: specifying database schemas, API contracts, and frontend component breakdowns that mentored engineers built against, then reviewing the code they shipped, managing deployments and releases, and acting as the bridge between engineering and non-technical staff.

Writing specs turned out to be a different job from writing code. When you write the spec, correctness is between you and a colleague's next two weeks. A vague schema decision becomes someone else's confusing Tuesday. I learned to write decisions explicit, boundaries named, ambiguity hunted down early.

Then the interim lead role made it explicit: planning, reviewing, unblocking, deciding. I onboarded new engineers on the architecture — which forced me to articulate choices I'd made by instinct — and directly mentored two interns. The first time I was responsible for someone else's growth inside a system, not just the system.

Alongside it, I conducted roughly 55 technical interviews covering system design and culture fit. Fifty-five candidates is fifty-five mirrors. My working answer to "what makes an engineer ready": reasoning out loud, honesty about the edges of what they know, judgment under uncertainty. Not encyclopedic knowledge. I hold myself to that same standard now.

I left with something no course grants: the experience of being trusted with a team before feeling fully ready, and finding out that readiness is mostly built by exactly that.
