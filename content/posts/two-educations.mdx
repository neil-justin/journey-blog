I never got one education. I got two at the same time — AISAT College and the software industry — and they kept interrupting each other.

The degree gave me the map. A curriculum that builds on purpose, from first-year precision (Computer Programming 1, Discrete Structures) through structure (Data Structures, OOP, Automata) and systems (Operating Systems, Networks) to synthesis (Software Engineering, the thesis). No job would have taught me automata. No internship hands you a plan of everything an engineer should eventually know. The degree's gift was breadth with a plan.

The industry gave me the mileage. Career Team LLC: a multi-tenant platform, thousands of real users, real uptime. Thorne Consulting: specs end-to-end, code review, two interns, around 55 interviews as interim lead. Industry's teaching method is consequences. A slow query is a person waiting. A vague spec is a colleague's wasted week. I know which lessons I'd have skipped if consequences hadn't made me sit through them.

Neither could do the other's job. Knowledge says how a system can work. Judgment says what it should cost and whose problem it is today. The two rescued each other constantly: second-year data structures pointed straight at my production MongoDB fix, and production experience made SE 1's diagrams obvious instead of abstract. When I wondered, somewhere around interview #40, what "ready" even means, the answer used both languages — reasoning you can explain, limits you admit.

I started here unable to write a line of code. First program, first all-nighter, first side project, first production bug, first spec written for someone else, first intern, first lead role. Each looked small at the time. Stacked in order, they're the distance.

The degree without the work is theory without consequence. The work without the degree is mileage without a map. I got to build both at once, and I didn't understand how unusual that was until I sat down to write this.

This blog closes where the journey stands: 4th year, thesis ahead, a working career already running alongside. Graduation is next. After that I genuinely don't know, and for the first time that feels fine.
