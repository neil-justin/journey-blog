# Two Educations

I never received one education. I received two, at the same time, from AISAT College and the software industry — and they kept interrupting each other.

**The degree gave me the map.** A curriculum that builds deliberately, course by course, from first-year precision (Computer Programming 1, Discrete Structures) through structure (Data Structures and Algorithms, OOP, Automata Theory) and systems (Operating Systems, Networks) to synthesis (Software Engineering, the thesis). No job would have taught me automata. No internship hands you a plan for everything an engineer should eventually know. Breadth with a plan — that's the degree's gift.

**The industry gave me the mileage.** Career Team LLC: a multi-tenant platform, thousands of real users, real uptime. Thorne Consulting: planning features end-to-end, reviewing others' code, mentoring two interns, around 55 interviews as interim lead. Industry's method is consequences — a slow query is a person waiting, a vague spec is a colleague's wasted week. Lessons that arrive wearing consequences stick.

**Neither could do the other's job.** Knowledge says how a system can work. Judgment says what it should cost, whose problem it is, whether today is the day to build it. The two kept rescuing each other along the way: second-year data structures pointed straight at my production MongoDB fix, and production experience made SE 1's diagrams obvious instead of abstract. When I interviewed 55 candidates wondering what "ready" means, the answer turned out to be written in both languages — reasoning you can explain, limits you admit, judgment under uncertainty.

I started here unable to write a line of code. Along the way: my first program, my first all-nighter, my first side project, my first production bug, my first spec written for someone else, my first intern, my first lead role. Each looked small at the time. Stacked in order, they're the whole distance.

The degree without the work is theory without consequence. The work without the degree is mileage without a map. I got to build both at once, into one career, and I didn't fully understand how rare that is until writing this.

This blog closes where the journey stands: 4th year, thesis ahead, and a working career already running alongside. The horizon in front of me is graduation — but this blog has taught me that horizons are mostly doorways. Thank you for traveling the whole way with me.
