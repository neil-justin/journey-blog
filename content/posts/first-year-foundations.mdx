I came to AISAT College having never written a line of code. I picked CS mostly because I liked computers and it sounded employable. So did almost everyone in the room.

First program was in CC 102: print your name, then add two numbers, then a loop. I typed the examples by hand instead of copying the file, which I felt smug about for about a week. The first time a program compiled and still did the wrong thing, I sat there stuck for an hour because I didn't know debugging was a thing you could do on purpose. Nobody tells you that. You find it in a forum post at 1am.

The all-nighter people joke about: I had a machine problem due and spent the evening before it rewriting everything from scratch because my first approach "felt ugly." It got a passing grade and I got a lesson about scope that no course taught.

Discrete Structures 1 humbled me. I bombed the first quiz on logic and spent the rest of the semester catching up on sets and proofs. At the time it was just the course I was worst at. Second year's Data Structures felt survivable largely because of it, which is a thing I only noticed later.

The rest of the year — Introduction to Computing, Programming Languages, the gen-ed subjects — filled in around that. If I compress it: I learned that being wrong is information. The program tells you where you miscalculated and the fix is usually smaller than the fear of it. I didn't believe that yet in first year. I believe it now.
