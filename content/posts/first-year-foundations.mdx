# 1st Year — Foundations

I arrived at AISAT College having never written a line of code. So did almost everyone. That was the first thing first year taught me: everyone is confused at the start, and the confusion is the work.

My first programs were trivial — print a line, add two numbers, loop ten times. What I remember is the first time a program did exactly what I told it to instead of what I meant. That gap between intent and instruction turned out to be the real subject of the entire degree. Every course after it is a variation on that one lesson.

Discrete Structures 1 looked like a math course to survive rather than enjoy. It took a year to appreciate: logic, sets, proofs are the grammar of computing, and second year's Data Structures felt familiar largely because of it. I didn't believe that in first year. I believe it now.

The rest — Introduction to Computing, Programming Languages, the general education subjects — filled in the background. If I compress the year into one honest sentence: I learned that being wrong is information, not failure. The program tells you exactly where you miscalculated, and the fix is usually smaller than the fear of it.

Everything after this year assumed I learned these lessons well. I think I did — mostly by breaking things first.
