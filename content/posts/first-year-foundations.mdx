# 1st Year — Foundations

I started Computer Science at AISAT College without ever having written a line of code. That fact shaped the whole first year.

## Computer Programming 1: the first real conversation

Everything began with CC 102, Computer Programming 1. My first programs were trivial — print a line, add two numbers, loop ten times. But I remember the specific feeling of the first time a program did exactly what I told it to instead of what I meant. That gap, between intent and instruction, turned out to be the actual subject of the entire degree. Every course after it was a variation on that one lesson.

What I took from Programming 1 was not the language. It was the discipline of being precise. The compiler does not grade on effort. Either the logic holds or it does not, and no amount of wanting it to work changes anything. I found that harsh at first. Somewhere in that year I started finding it honest.

## Discrete Structures 1: learning to think in pieces

DS 101, Discrete Structures 1, was the other half of the year. It looked like a math course and I initially treated it like one to survive rather than enjoy — sets, logic, proofs, counting. It took me longer to appreciate why it was in the curriculum.

Discrete math is the grammar of computing. Logic gates, database conditions, loop invariants, the shape of an algorithm — all of it is discrete mathematics wearing a costume. I did not fully believe that in first year. I believed it by second year, when Data Structures started feeling familiar instead of foreign, and I eventually understood that Discrete Structures was why.

## What the year actually taught

The rest of first year — Introduction to Computing, Programming Languages, the general education subjects — filled in the background. But if I compress the year into one honest sentence: I learned how to be wrong in a way I could fix.

Before CS, being wrong felt like failing. After a year of debugging, being wrong became information. The program tells you exactly where you miscalculated, and the fix is usually smaller than the fear of it. That reframing is the most valuable thing first year gave me, and it cost a few all-nighters to earn.

Everything after this year assumed I learned these lessons well. I think I did — slowly, and mostly by breaking things first.
