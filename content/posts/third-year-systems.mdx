# 3rd Year — Systems

Third year was the heaviest academic year I had, and it ended in a way I didn't expect.

Operating Systems retired a picture I'd been carrying since first year: the OS as weather, something that just happens to my programs. After OS 101 I could see the machinery — processes, scheduling, memory. The idea that stayed with me most was concurrency: many actors, shared resources, no global clock. Working on a multi-tenant platform years later, that question — what happens when two things touch the same resource at once? — has never once been irrelevant.

Networks and Communications extended the idea across machines. The internet is agreements all the way down, most of them older than me. And distributed systems fail gracefully in diagrams and chaotically in reality — latency, partial failure, retries were textbook concerns here, then concrete ones the first time my code met a real user on a real connection.

Application Development and Emerging Technologies pulled the year together toward complete applications. Around this era I built MarkNote — a MERN-stack note app with Docker — deliberately practicing the full path: data model, API, frontend, deployment. It's on GitHub, and it was the closest thing I'd made to a real product.

This post ends where it does for a reason. Halfway through this year, while still working through my degree, I started my first software engineering job. The classroom didn't stop being important. It stopped being enough.

That story is next.
