# What Shipping Taught Me

Two jobs compressed a lot of education into a short time. The stories are in the previous posts; this is the distillation.

**Done is a performance word.** In school, done meant "works on the test cases." In production, done means working in front of people who didn't write it and don't care how it works. The configurable workforce forms at Career Team taught me a feature is done when a client uses it in a way I never imagined — real usage is the only test suite that never lies.

**Performance problems are data problems.** The slow MongoDB queries behind the admin dashboard weren't a UI issue, a framework issue, or a server issue. They were a data-shape issue. Second-year Data Structures and Algorithms was right, and it took real users waiting on a real dashboard to prove it.

**Ambiguity is the actual job.** School problems arrive fully specified. Real work doesn't. The most valuable thing I did at either company — specs at Thorne, the developer↔product bridge at Career Team — was turning vague intentions into precise decisions before those intentions became rework.

**Leadership is service made visible.** Reviewing code carefully, unblocking an intern, onboarding a new engineer: none of it looked like leadership from the inside. It looked like making other people's work possible. That's what it is.

**Interviews are mirrors.** Around 55 interviews at Thorne plus candidate interviews at Career Team gave me an external standard for what competence looks like: calm reasoning, admitted limits, structure under pressure.

**Deadlines are honest.** Releases, clients, and teammates depend on the date. The skill isn't panic — it's deciding what done-enough means, cutting deliberately, and communicating the tradeoff early.

Software is a social object: built with people, for people, against time. The engineering is the easy half of that sentence.
