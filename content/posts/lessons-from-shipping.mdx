The two jobs are in the previous posts. This is the residue.

The MongoDB thing. The Career Team dashboard was slow — eight, ten seconds a page. I was sure it was the frontend. Two days of memoizing and splitting requests, nothing. Then an explain() on a profiles query showed COLLSCAN: we queried by tenantId + createdAt but had an index on tenantId only. One compound index later, under a second. I got an A in Data Structures and still needed two days and a wrong guess to remember the course existed.

Deadlines. My first release, I treated the date like a school deadline. My lead asked on day three what was getting cut. Not whether I'd finish — what was getting cut. School projects are scoped so you can finish. Real ones aren't, so deciding what "done enough" means, early, out loud, is part of the job.

Thorne taught me what vague specs cost. I left notification behavior undefined in one spec because it seemed obvious. It wasn't. The engineer built the wrong call, cost a week to rewrite. The fix wasn't longer specs. It was answering questions before the next standup instead of after the demo.

The interviews. Roughly 55 candidates at Thorne, and I noticed I kept grading people against a version of me that never pauses or says "I'm not sure." The ones I pushed to hire thought out loud and admitted their edges. I interview myself that way now. Slower to answer, better to work with. I think.

One more, smaller. Real software stays yours after you ship it. A forms feature I built came back six months later — a client had built an entire intake workflow out of it, one I never designed for. Assignments end at submission. This doesn't. Not a complaint. Just the thing I wasn't ready for.
